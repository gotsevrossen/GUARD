Someone tried to sign in to the file server 14 times in four minutes from 10.0.4.66, and every attempt failed. That pattern looks like a program guessing passwords rather than a person mistyping their own.

Today, 11:07 AM ▾

Get help now

Contact your IT provider or a security professional now.

This could be serious, and LightHouse can’t be sure what happened. Call your IT provider, a managed security provider or an incident-response firm, and tell them about this alert. While you wait:

  1. Don’t delete files, programs or logs. Whoever investigates will need them.
  2. Don’t reply to, contact or pay anyone demanding money.
  3. Write down what you saw: the time, anything on screen, and anything unusual.
  4. Disconnect the affected device from the network (unplug its cable or turn off its Wi-Fi) if that won’t stop critical work. Leave it switched on.

What this means

Someone tried to sign in to the file server 14 times in four minutes from 10.0.4.66, and every attempt failed. That pattern looks like a program guessing passwords rather than a person mistyping their own.

LightHouse’s suggestion — check with your IT provider first

  1. Block 10.0.4.66 at your router or firewall. That stops the attempts while you look into it.
  2. Find out what the device at that address is. Your router’s list of connected devices shows its name.
  3. Change the password on every account it tried. Pick one nobody else knows.
